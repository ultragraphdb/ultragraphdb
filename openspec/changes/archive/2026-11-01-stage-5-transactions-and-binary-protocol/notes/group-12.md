# Group 12: measurements

`ultragraphdb_tool_bench`. Task 12.1, the stage-4 workload under `Durability::Filesystem` as the baseline; task 12.5,
the write volume of the write-ahead log beside fjall's journal; task 12.8, the results in the overview's "Measured,
stage 5" (` zero-copy-binary-protocol.md`, "Measurements at the exit"), naming the machine and the drive, with the
design's risk "Every round is written twice" pointing at them. Tasks 12.2, 12.3, 12.4, 12.6, 12.7 and 12.5's indexer
lag are in `openspec/notes/backlog.md`.

## The bench

12.1 runs `store_bench apply` unchanged, with `--durability filesystem`, beside `--durability process` on the same
machine in the same hour.

12.5 takes a new binary, `wal_bench`, in the same crate: the stage-4 workload through stage 5's commit path. A source
thread releases `store_bench apply`'s commits (`CommitShape`, 1,000 hubs, 10 relation types, seed 1) at the offered
rate, at most 256 in flight beyond those answered, as the apply run does; the commit thread begins each as a
read-write `Transaction` at the watermark, writes its changeset, commits it to a `Prepared` and hands it to the
`Pipeline` over `Wal<CompioStorage>` and the changeset applier over fjall (the setup `store_bench` opens, with
`Durability::Process`, so the batch the pipeline writes with `PersistMode::Buffer` reaches fjall's journal unsynced);
a round in flight is synced (`Pipeline::sync`), which applies it and closes the next over the requests waiting. The
round bounds are `WalConfig`'s for the `--plp` flag. No checkpoint runs (the pipeline has none; backlog item 7), so
the log's segments hold every round and their length is the log's write volume. Rollups do not run: they write fjall
outside the log. The report counts the replies (committed, aborted), the rounds (the log's committed position), each
round's sync and apply time, the log's segments and superblock, the store's files, and fjall's journals measured as
their bytes up to the last byte that is not zero, before the first commit and after the last: fjall creates a journal
at 64 MiB (`fjall::journal::writer::PRE_ALLOCATED_BYTES`) and appends into it, so neither its length nor its allocated
blocks are its contents. On APFS the log's segments allocate more blocks than their length (8.35 MB for 7.39 MB in a
3 s trial), so the log is measured by length, which is what it wrote: a round's frame padded to 4,096-byte blocks.

```text
wal_bench --dir <path> --rate <commits/s> [--relations 1] [--seconds 30] [--cache-mb 128] [--plp off|on]
```

Files: `src/bin/wal_bench.rs`; `src/types/pipeline_args/mod.rs` (`PipelineArgs`, its `parse` over `command`'s flag
helpers, made `pub(crate)`); `src/utils/pipeline_run.rs` (`run_pipeline`, with an in-src test module for the report's
arithmetic); `BenchError::Commit`, the pipeline's, a transaction's or the log's error as text; the crate's
documentation; `Cargo.toml` (the `wal_bench` binary; `ultragraphdb_traits_log`, `ultragraphdb_transactions_commit`,
`ultragraphdb_transactions_log`, `ultragraphdb_transactions_txn` and `compio`); `BUILD.bazel` (`:wal_bench`, a
`rust_binary` tagged `bench`, as `history_bench` and `read_set_bench` are). Tests:
`tests/types/pipeline_args_tests.rs` (4: the defaults, every flag, the lower bounds, each refusal with its text),
`tests/utils/pipeline_run_tests.rs` (3: a 1 s run at 10 commits a second checked by its invariants, a run with the
flag on, a directory that exists refused), two in-src tests of the report (every figure by hand; a run of nothing
divides by nothing), and the new variant in `tests/errors/bench_error_tests.rs`. The expected values: the usage text
and the defaults the crate's documentation states; the block of 4,096 bytes the change's design states; the report's
figures computed by hand in the tests' comments. The tests were written beside the code rather than before it, and
their audit against planted defects is task 11.8's mutation run over the crate (agent M3).

`bazel test //ultragraphdb_tool/ultragraphdb_tool_bench:all`: `ultragraphdb_tool_bench_tests` 68 passed,
`ultragraphdb_tool_bench_src_tests` 5 passed.

## The runs

```text
bazel build -c opt //ultragraphdb_tool/ultragraphdb_tool_bench:store_bench //ultragraphdb_tool/ultragraphdb_tool_bench:wal_bench
store_bench apply --dir <fresh> --rate <14500|50000> --relations <1|50> --seconds 30 --durability <filesystem|process>
wal_bench --dir <fresh> --rate <14500|50000> --relations <1|50> --seconds 30 --plp <off|on>
```

Each binary was copied out of `bazel-out/darwin_arm64-opt/bin` and run from a shell script, one configuration after
another, each in a fresh directory under the session's scratch directory on the internal drive, removed after its
report; the 1-minute load average was read before and after each run (`sysctl vm.loadavg`). Series a and b of each,
and one series of `wal_bench --plp on`, on 2026-11-01 between 04:36 and 04:58 UTC.

**Machine and drive.** MacBook Pro (Mac15,8), Apple M3 Max, 16 cores, 128 GiB, macOS 27.2 (26B123); APPLE SSD AP8192Z,
8 TB, APFS, on the internal Apple Fabric bus; power-loss protection undocumented by Apple. Native `aarch64-apple-darwin`
builds, not OrbStack (stages 2 and 4 ran in OrbStack's Linux VM, `debian:bookworm-slim`, glibc).

**The load.** The stage's mutation and fuzzing agents shared the 16 cores throughout, at 1-minute load averages of 96
to 197. The rates are CPU-starved: stage 4 measured 12,215 commits a second at 14,500 offered and one relation per
commit, idle in OrbStack; here the same run commits 1,718–2,297. They compare with each other only roughly and with
stage 4 not at all. The bytes per relation do not depend on the load; the commits per round do, through the time a
round takes, except where a round closes on its byte bound.

**The syncs.** fjall's journal syncs through `std::fs::File::sync_all` (`fjall-3.1.13/src/journal/writer.rs`), which
the standard library implements on Apple targets as `fcntl(fd, F_FULLFSYNC)` (`library/std/src/sys/fs/unix.rs`,
`os_fsync` and `os_datasync`): `Durability::Filesystem`'s per-commit sync flushes the drive. The write-ahead log's
`CompioFile::sync` is compio's `sync_data`, which compio-driver 0.12.5 runs on macOS as a blocking rustix `fsync`
(`build.rs` sets `datasync` for Unix without Apple and `aio` for FreeBSD and illumos alone; `sys/op/fs/poll.rs`;
rustix 1.1.6's `fsync` calls `libc::fsync`, its `F_FULLFSYNC` a separate call): the log's
per-round sync does not flush the drive's cache, as `CompioStorage`'s documentation and the design's macOS risk state.
The probe below measured both on this drive, in the same hour and under the same load, with Python's `os.fsync` and
`fcntl.fcntl(fd, fcntl.F_FULLFSYNC)` over 200 appends of 4 KiB each, twice:

| Sync | p50 | p99 | Mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fsync` | 40 µs, 46 µs | 148 µs, 363 µs | 99 µs, 56 µs |
| `F_FULLFSYNC` | 11.7 ms, 8.2 ms | 33.5 ms, 33.8 ms | 13.0 ms, 9.7 ms |

## Results

The tables in the overview's "Measured, stage 5" are the ranges over the runs below.


### Findings

- **The log writes 1.06–1.13 times the journal's bytes** for the same commits: 1.13 at one relation per commit
  (2,002–2,010 bytes a relation against the journal's 1,780), 1.06–1.07 at 50 (1,100–1,106 against 1,035–1,036). D1's
  "the write volume of commits roughly doubles" holds: 2.06–2.13 times the journal's alone. The log is trimmed only
  at checkpoints, which the pipeline does not yet run, so in these runs it also held its bytes on disk.
- **Round sizes.** At 50 relations a commit's record is about 55 KB and a round closes on its byte bound: 18.8–18.9
  commits in 1 MiB (power-loss protection off), 4.0 in 256 KiB (on). At one relation a round held 83–85 commits with
  the flag on or off, within both bounds; the bench's limit of 256 commits in flight bounds it. The padding to 4,096
  bytes is then at most 2.4 % of a round; a round of one 1-relation commit would double its record.
- **The baseline's cost is the flush.** `Durability::Filesystem` commits 32–37 one-relation commits a second, one
  `F_FULLFSYNC` each (8.2–11.7 ms at the median in the probe), and 17–23 commits of 50; `Durability::Process`, unsynced,
  1,669–2,297 and 101–117. The pipeline, one unflushed `fsync` and one fjall batch a round, commits 2,982–4,411 and
  92–153 (4,602–7,641 relations a second). The pipeline's rate under a flushed sync per round is not measured here: the
  log's storage calls `fsync` on macOS, and the design measures durability on Linux.
- **No aborts** in any pipeline run: each commit writes new nodes, and the reads it records are of keys no other
  commit writes.

### Left for later runs

- The log's sync under `F_FULLFSYNC`, and both paths on Linux with power-loss protection and without: task 12.4's
  sync cost moved to the backlog (item 7), and OrbStack was not running for this group.
- An idle machine: the other agents' load was not under this group's control. A rerun when the machine is idle, with
  `store_bench` and `wal_bench` as above, gives rates comparable with stage 4's.
