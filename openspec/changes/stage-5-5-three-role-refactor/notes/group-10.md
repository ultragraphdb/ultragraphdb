# Group 10: the client against the cluster

Tasks 10.1, 10.2 and 10.3, against the core's test server. 10.4 and 10.5 are open.

**10.1 The Ultra Style lift.** Bazel before the lift: `client_core` unit 125, bdd 13 scenarios and 26 steps; `client_tokio` unit 24; `client_compio` unit 20. `cargo fmt` changed nothing, and `cargo clippy --all-targets --all-features -- -D warnings` reported nothing. The lift changes no behaviour: 84 `#[must_use]`, the shells' cancellation statement, their event loops' and `read_frame`'s bounds, the core's plane, one `Exception (Ultra rule 7)` on `transmit`'s stamp, whose error only a frame shorter than its header gives, and the reason on the test server's `Arc`. Bazel after it: the same four counts. It lands with the group's changes, since this wave commits nothing; its diff is 738 lines, and the user can commit that step apart only by staging it by hand. With the lints the table holds back, the three `src/` trees showed casts of wall-clock microseconds and arithmetic on counters; the two that matter, an `Instant` plus a configured timeout and plus a server's `expires`, panicked on a value from outside and are behaviour, so they went to 10.3 as `checked_add`, each with a test. Rule 5's density is below two checks a function in the core, as it was in every crate this change lifted; 10.3's paths add theirs.

**10.2 The scenarios.** Observed failing before the implementation, under Bazel, with the surface's new bodies `unimplemented!()`: 40 scenarios, 17 passed and 23 failed, 61 steps, 38 passed and 23 failed. The 27 new ones are in the core's `features/`, `wire-protocol/`, `transactions/` and `quad-reads/`, bound in `tests/bdd_steps/`; "A retried commit commits once", "A retry of a session evicted meanwhile is unknown", "A late frame reaches the client" and "A refusal after rows fails the read" passed from the start, since the code already did what they say from a lost read's side, and they pin it. Each shell has a `bdd` target of its own for what a shell decides: "A long read flows without a manual grant", "A TLS client reaches the endpoint", "A clear-text client is refused by a TLS endpoint". Those three were written with the shells' TLS, whose types they need to compile, so they were not seen failing. A check over the spec and the feature files found every scenario verbatim.

Bound elsewhere, or left: "A Recall reaching no answer stays in doubt" is bound here for the client's half, the test server playing the frontend's code 17; the frontend binds its half. "Any other refusal of a Recall stays in doubt", "A stored reply outranks every other Recall answer", "A session commits through two partitions", "A read with a token sees the commit", "A causally later commit is not stamped earlier", "A named read version takes nothing from a token", "An upsert decides in commit order" and the rest of quad-reads' "A read's transaction names its level" need partitions, and are 10.4's end to end. What the client contributes to each, the token on the Begin and the number on the resend, is pinned here by unit tests.

**10.3 The dialling side.** `ClientCore::receive` asks `place` first: step 3 by the frame's command on its stream, before the chunks, the sequence and the protocol, so a frame on a stream at or above the next, or by a command its request's "Answered by" does not list, is `CommandNotServed` and closes the connection, whatever else is wrong with it. A Welcome on another stream and any other command in the handshake go the same way, in the core rather than the transport's `ClientHandshake`, which `RoleClient` also uses and which keeps `unexpected_command` for roles. A frame on a stream this side ended is viewed, so it takes steps 4 to 15, and dropped whatever its command; that includes a frame behind a read's `LAST` or Error still in its inbox, and one on a transaction's stream already refused. A Result stays unviewed in the inbox until it is read, its order checked on arrival, as `ReceivedFrame::check_order` provides, so a frame is checksummed once.

One counter per session serves `commit`, `drop_command`, `direct_write` and `conditional_write`, each taking `impl Into<Option<Session>>`, so a plain `Session` and `None` both read naturally. `next_number` is the one place a number is taken, and `Resend`, boxed since a pending request is frequent and a body in doubt rare, is what goes again: a Commit names only its session and request, a Drop's and a one-frame write's frame holds what it does. A first code 17 is `InDoubt` at once, with the refusal its cause, and keeps its body in the session; `resolve` sends it again; a resend's 14 or 17 waits the retry delay, bounded by `commit_attempts`. `settle_commit` is §11.5's table, and `RetriesExhausted` is gone into `InDoubt`. A commit whose frame never left on any connection answers `ConnectionLost` at once, since its transaction went with its connection, where before it waited to be sent again, which could only earn a code 18. `ever_sent` is what tells it from a retry queued at a loss, which keeps its attempts.

The read's timeout is `read_timeout`, per stream: the later of its progress and its transaction's deadline, plus the larger of the client's timeout and 30 s; a read with a frame to read or no credit runs none. Everything else keeps the client's own timeout over the connection's last progress.

`ErrorCode::runs_again(RefusedRequest)` and `RunsAgain` are the wire's, and `ClientError::runs_again` adds a commit in doubt; `Refused` carries the kind of request refused. `ErrorCode::retryable` stays, since the server's tests and the commit step read it as the table's column, code 17 apart. TLS is `TlsDial` over any `Connect`, in both shells; tokio's `TokioTlsStream` drives the transport's `TlsSession` by polls, as `CompioTlsStream` does by awaits. `TlsSession` now sets no limit on rustls's buffer: a `send` past 64 KiB failed, which a frame reaches, on the compio stream and the frontend's alike, and a test in `transport_tls` and each shell's now send one past it. `grant` takes `NonZeroU32` and the time, and no shell offers it; the core's own Credit is `ClientConfig::credit`, at least 1. A Credit or Cancel for a read whose end sits in its inbox is not sent. `resume_session` returns a `Result`, refusing session 0, request 0, and a session held with a request in flight or in doubt, and keeps a held token. `Committed` carries its token and the node each result names.

Decisions the text left. A Begin's deadline is measured here from its reply, later than the server's from its arrival, so the client's timer can only run long. Code 5 on a Drop or one-frame write is the caller's to send again, the number kept: §9.4 says what runs again, not who, and the client already auto-resends only what is in doubt or busy. A resent Commit refused by anything but 12, 13, 14, 17 and 18 stays in doubt with its cause and no automatic resend, since the frontend's Recall maps those to 17 and such a refusal says nothing of the commit, a permission refused on the resend among them. `direct_write` and `conditional_write` refuse a nonzero `principal` before they send, naming the field as the endpoint would. A session's token keeps the first of an equal epoch. A commit reply naming another session stays `ReplyMismatch`, the request number kept, as it was.

Counts under Bazel: `client_core` unit 162, bdd 40 scenarios; `client_tokio` unit 28, bdd 3; `client_compio` unit 23, bdd 3; `transport_tls` and `protocol_wire` each gained one test. Tests that pinned the old rules changed with them: the fault suite's probe now goes on a stream the client ended, a duplicate of a Welcome or of a Begin's reply is refused by its command, and "a grant of no frames" is gone, since the type rules it out. `PROTOCOL.md` is unchanged.

What is left. 10.4 runs every scenario above against the binaries. In both shells a deadline that fires without giving the connection up, a credential's renewal or a busy commit's resend, still cuts the read and reconnects: a tokio read can be made to survive it, since `read_buf` loses nothing, but a compio read cannot, as a dropped owned-buffer read may lose what it took, and writing while a read is out needs the transport to split its stream. Nothing here depends on it, since a read's own timeout fires only when the read is lost, but "A credential is renewed on the open connection" holds of the core alone until that is done. A session resumed in a new client starts with no token, since `resume_session` takes none; a caller who kept one sets it on its `Begin`. Mutation testing is 10.5.
