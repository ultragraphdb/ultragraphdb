Every task group follows `openspec/protocols/tdd-protocol.md`'s five phases, in order: (1) the group's public surface
compiling with bare `unimplemented!()` bodies, whose warnings are ignored until phase 4, where any left name an
overlooked body (`clippy::unimplemented` warns by design); (2) the
full suite written against it and observed failing for that reason, its run and test count recorded in the group's
notes (`notes/group-N.md`): the group's scenarios verbatim as Gherkin in the crate's `features/<capability>/` bound by
its `bdd` target with `fail_on_skipped`, unit tests for the corner cases enumerated in advance, and every error variant
constructed through the public API, each expected value from a source independent of the code; (3) the suite's inputs
reviewed against the corner-case enumeration; (4) the implementation, judged by the suite alone; (5)
`scripts/mutants.sh <crate> --diff <base>` once per crate over every line the group changed in it, at most three runs per crate (the
diff, one fix pass, one `--re` re-run of the mutants it killed), survivors killed by a test or recorded in
`.cargo/mutants.toml` with their measurement, and any still open after the third run listed in the group's notes for
task 12.1 — never `cargo mutants` directly; and, for a group that decodes bytes from a client, a peer or the disk, its fuzz targets and seeds written
and built, while the runs of `scripts/fuzz.sh` happen once, in group 12. No defects are planted by hand. Before any of its crates'
behaviour changes, a group lifts each existing crate it touches into `openspec/protocols/ULTRA_STYLE.md`: `make format`
and `make fix` leaving it unchanged and reporting nothing, the rules no lint checks reviewed and recorded, the suite
unchanged and green with the same count under Bazel, all in a commit of its own; every crate this change creates
conforms from its first commit. The lints the table holds commented out stay out of this change. A lint is silenced
only with the user's consent. Bazel is the test gate: a group is done when `make test` passes; the user commits after
each group. Removing files waits for the user's approval at the task that removes them. Group 6b is the one exception
to the Gherkin rule: it has no crate and is built by `lake` outside Bazel, as stage 5's `RoundModel` was, so each of its
behavioural scenarios is a Lean theorem named after the scenario, while "The placement model builds without gaps" is
checked by `lake build PlacementModel` succeeding with no `sorry` in the library.
